## ARC-145 — A sit point is the world's; how a seated person is drawn is presentation, decided once for both clients

**Date** 2026-10-21 · **Status** accepted (S33-f's frozen design, revision 2, 2026-10-19; implemented in PR
`mvp0/pr-s33f`) · **Relates to** `ARC-147` (posture), `ARC-307` (furnishings), `ARC-172`,
[`CORE_CONCEPTS.md`](../CORE_CONCEPTS.md) §6.4, `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-33-lakeside-3d.md` §4.5
(SD-S33-7), `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-34-posture.md` §§5.1, 8.1, 8.2 (SD-S34-1…4, SD-S34-8; Q-S34-3,
Q-S34-9), `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s34c-world-sit-points.md` (Q-S34c-2),
`.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s33f-interiors-sit-points.md` §§5.1, 5.4 (the design)

**Rewritten.** Step-33 assigned this id to "sit points as presentation data and the shared rule": a client
would know where people may sit and decide, by a rule of its own, who looks seated. Step-34 (frozen
2026-10-18) made sitting the world's: a sit point is semantic place data owned by the `posture` System Pack,
and a seated person is a disclosed fact. This record states the step-33 decision in step-34's shape; nothing
of step-33's acceptance is dropped (F-S33f-1).

**Problem.** Lakeside's people should look seated on its benches and at its long table, in both reference
clients, the same people at the same seats. The world must own where a person may sit and who sits; the
client owns only the pose. Lakeside does not yet enable `posture` (S34-c does, Q-S33f-1), so for now the
clients also need a way to draw sitting without the world's say.

**Choice.**

1. **A sit point is the world's**: `posture`'s `SitPoint { key, at, facing, group? }` in a place file's
   `sit-points:` section (`ARC-147`), `at` on free floor at the seat (or, for a chair at a table, behind
   it on the aisle side, Q-S34c-2). No client file is the authority on where a person may sit.
2. **Its depiction is presentation**, one file per world in the 3D Presentation Pack:
   `sit_points/<world-key>.yaml` binds a point by its place's tags and its key to the drawn seat —
   `seat_height_m` (the seat's surface), `seat_offset_m` (signed: the pelvis moves from `at` against the
   facing, back onto a bench or forward onto a chair), and `prop`, the furnishing whose seat it is
   (`ARC-307`). A seated body's own seat rests on `seat_height_m`: the client measures, from the body's
   skinned vertices, how far its buttocks hang below its hips and seats it by that (`SeatContact`), one rule
   for every body.
3. **One decision, shared.** `clients/shared/sitting/sit_rule.gd` (`MineWorldSitRule`), taken by both
   clients as `res://mineworld_sitting`, decides who is drawn seated where, from the observation alone:
   - where the place discloses `sit-points`, a person is drawn seated iff their disclosed `posture-state`
     names a point (step-34 SD-S34-8);
   - otherwise, step-33's rule as the fallback: at the nearest point whose fallback `at` lies within
     0.40 m of the person, once they have not moved for 2 s of world time, unless a person with a lower
     entity id is drawn there already.
   Neither client holds a copy of these constants (`client_rules.rs`, I-f-6), and both are checked to draw
   the same person on a contested seat (`client_sit_rule.rs`, AC-f-4).
4. **The fallback positions are temporary.** Until a world enables `posture`, its depiction file also
   carries each point's `fallback: { at, facing }`. They are fixed once (Lakeside's in the S33-f design
   §5.2) and moved into the world's place files verbatim by S34-c, which removes them here (`ARC-172`).

**Consequences.** Lakeside shows sitting from S33-f on, through the fallback, and from the server once
S34-c enables `posture` there; the pose code does not change between the two. The 2D client decides the same
seats but draws them only from S34-e. A world with no sit-point depiction file has no fallback sitting and
loses nothing else. The fallback rule is a pose decision, never a fact: it moves nobody and asks the server
for nothing.

**Rejected.** A client-side sit rule as the authority (step-33's original shape): it would let two clients
disagree with the world once `posture` exists. Writing Lakeside's sit points into world data in S33-f
(Q-S33f-1 (C)): a second re-baseline of Lakeside's digests beside the one S34-c makes (`ARC-172`).
