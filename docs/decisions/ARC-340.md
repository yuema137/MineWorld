## ARC-340 — A place's capacity holds the people who can reach it, not the whole population

**Date** 2026-10-20 · **Status** accepted (operator ruling relayed by the coordinator, 2026-10-20;
implemented in PR `fix/bodies-reachable-capacity`) · **Relates to** `ARC-39` (amended), `ARC-26`,
[`MODULE_SPEC.md`](../MODULE_SPEC.md) `body:`, `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-11-bodies.md`
SD-B4 and F-D8 (superseded in part)

**Problem.** `bodies` refused a world at genesis unless every shaped place's floor held the entire
population: `4 · (people − 1) + 1` points of its 650 mm grid, plus what its objects block (step-11
F-D8). A world whose places are separated — tiled blocks, several settlements, a generated town of
100 people in nine café blocks (S28-g, D-g-8) — was refused although nobody in one block can ever stand
in another block's café: "station-tickets-01 … 54 points … 24 people needs 93".

**Choice.**

1. The count a shaped place's capacity must hold, at `place-shaped` (SD-B4) and at `object-placed`
   (SD-O5), is the **people who can reach it**: the living Persons whose presence is in a place of the
   place's connected component of the passage graph, plus every living Person with no presence yet.
   The formula is unchanged: `4 · (reach − 1) + 1` points, plus the objects' `blocks`.
2. Reach is derived from world data only: movement's `Passages`, the one item of that crate bodies
   already reads (`ARC-39` note 7). A person changes place only through a passage (`move`, `ARC-26`),
   and `passage-opened` always opens both ways, so the component is exactly where a person can go. A
   semantic passage (no doorway points) joins places like any other. Nothing names a world, a block or
   a place kind.
3. A person with no presence may arrive anywhere, so they count toward every place: the check stays
   conservative for them.
4. The refusal keeps its code, `bodies-capacity`, and its detail names the place, its points, the
   number of people who can reach it through passages, and the points that needs.

**The guarantee kept.** The capacity check exists for entry placement's last fallback (E3,
`entry.rs`): an arrival lands on the nearest free point of the place, and the check is what promises
one exists. Only someone who can reach a place can arrive in it, so counting the reachable set keeps
that promise for every arrival that can happen.

**Limitation.** As before (`ARC-39` note, point 3: "a world grown after genesis"), the check is made at
genesis. A passage opened later that joins two components, or a person created later, can exceed a
place's capacity; E3 then panics with its existing message. A runtime check is not decided here.

**Amends `ARC-39`** and supersedes step-11 F-D8's "against the whole world's population".

**Why.** The old rule made the capacity of the smallest room a ceiling on the size of every world, so
no world could grow by tiling or by adding separated places. Counting reach is the tightest bound the
world data already discloses, needs no new authoring, and keeps the one guarantee the check was for.
