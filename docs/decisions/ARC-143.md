## ARC-143 — Lakeside is set on Lake Cuyamaca's north shore, in presentation only, and its far fields are derived from USGS data

**Date** 2026-10-20 · **Status** accepted (PR S33-a design frozen 2026-10-18); implemented by S33-a · **Approved by** the operator's rulings Q-S33-1, Q-S33-2, Q-S33-9 and Q-S33-11 (2026-10-15) and the freeze of `pr-s33a-setting-far-field.md` (2026-10-18) · **Relates to** `ARC-102`…`ARC-104`, `ARC-107`, `ARC-144`, [`ART_DIRECTION.md`](../ART_DIRECTION.md) §4, [`MODULE_SPEC.md`](../MODULE_SPEC.md) §6.1.1 · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-33-lakeside-3d.md` §§3.1–3.4; `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s33a-setting-far-field.md` §§5.1–5.7

**Problem.** Lakeside's far fields were RL-e0's design content: a lake to the **north** of the lane at sea level
(App. B.2, B.3), with hills invented to close the circle. The world's own places put the shore and the pier on
the lane's **south** side, so the far field contradicted the world, and none of its numbers could be checked
against anything. A walkable world needs a horizon that a reviewer can hold against a map.

**Options considered.**

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| (a) Keep the design content and only turn the lake south | Declined: still invented relief; no reviewer can tell a right ridge from a wrong one |
| (b) A real setting declared by the world (a location in `worlds/lakeside`) | Declined (Q-S33-11): a location in simulation data would invite weather, calendar and light Systems to read it; the setting is how one Presentation Pack draws the world |
| (c) **A real setting in presentation only, derived reproducibly from public data** | **Chosen** |

**Choice.**

1. Lakeside is drawn as a village on the **north shore of Lake Cuyamaca** (Cuyamaca Reservoir), San Diego
   County. The far field's origin, the lane's centre, stands on the point `tools/far-field/lakeside/derive.py`
   chose by the design's site rule (§5.2) from the USGS NHD outline and USGS 3DEP elevations; the point,
   every candidate and the reason for the choice are recorded in `tools/far-field/lakeside/SOURCE.md`.
2. The location lives **only** in the Presentation Pack's far-field files
   (`presentation/mineworld-default/{3D,2D}/far_field/lakeside.yaml`). No file under `worlds/lakeside`, no
   calendar, no weather System and no kernel type learns it.
3. Every number in those files comes from `derive.py profile`, which runs offline from a committed cache of the
   USGS reads (NHD, GNIS, 3DEP through the Elevation Point Query Service) by RL-b C8's method (bearing and
   distance on the sphere; the elevation angle with earth curvature and refraction k = 0.13), and each carries
   its `source`. A value that cannot be cited says "approximate" and names who replaces it.
4. The lake lies **south** of the lane in both the 3D and the 2D file, and the two files' water bodies are the
   same body (AC-FF-4, strengthened by S33-a to compare `kind` and `level_m` as well).
5. The halt's railway is a **stated departure from history**: the San Diego, Cuyamaca and Eastern Railway
   (1889–1916) ended at Foster and never reached the lake. The station slot's `source` says so (Q-S33-2).
6. The references (`06_lake_trail.png`, `01_lakeside_promenade.png`) govern architecture, materials, light and
   mood; the setting governs terrain, peaks, water extent, vegetation and the far field. The references' snow
   peak, waterfall and far-shore spire are not copied (step-33 §3.3).

**Consequences.** A reviewer can check the south skyline (Cush-pii straight across the water, Cuyamaca Peak to
the south-west, North Peak behind the village) against the USGS "Cuyamaca Peak" 7.5′ quadrangle. S33-d builds the
near lake and terrain to these files' `level_m`, `shore_m` and `datum_m`; S33-h changes the 2D look, never the
lake's data. Another world gets a real setting the same way: a derivation tool and a cache beside its far field,
nothing in the client.

**Limitation accepted.** The visibility range is RL-e0's 80 km, marked approximate until the IMPROVE network's
Agua Tibia (AGTI1) mean is read. The Laguna Mountains sit at or just below the near crest on their bearings at
the cached sample spacing, so the far layer names none of them.

**Revisit** when Lakeside is moved, or when a second world with a real setting shows which part of
`derive.py` is a shared tool rather than Lakeside's.
