## DEP-161 — Prop, surface and town-body textures: Godot's built-in Basis Universal (UASTC + RDO by role); cast meshes unchanged

**Date** 2026-10-20 · **Status** accepted · **Approved by** operator ruling 2026-10-19 ("NPC models go in now, package size gets compressed later — now is later"), relayed by the primary session; extends `DEP-160` · **Relates to** `DEP-160`, `DEP-8`, `ARC-80`, [`REUSE_POLICY.md`](../REUSE_POLICY.md) · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-23-release.md` §18 (MS-Ra-2)

**Problem.** After `DEP-160` the S23 packager measured `3d.pck` at 364.8 MB compressed (hard limit 250) and the
macOS universal archive at 523.7 MB (hard limit 400). Of the pack, 211 MB were textures other than the cast's base
colours: the 213 slice-owned maps under `assets/textures/**` and `assets/models/**/textures/**` (BPTC, `compress/mode=2`,
owned by `slice_imports.py`) and the 16 town-body maps under `assets/characters/vitruvian/textures/` (Lossless, owned by
`patch_imports.py`). 72 MB were imported glTF scenes, 56 MB of them the 21 cast bodies.

**What a macOS universal pack holds.** Godot 4.7's macOS exporter asks for the `s3tc` and `bptc` features for the
universal and x86_64 architectures (Apple silicon samples BC formats on Metal), so a VRAM-compressed texture is in the
pack once, as BPTC, not twice. The ASTC copy that the packaging import makes (`D-Ra-6`) never reaches the pack. Basis
therefore saves by its supercompression, not by dropping a second copy.

**Options compared** (three props: `wooden_crate_01`, `wine_bottles_01`, `standing_chalkboard_01`, 21 maps; Godot 4.7.2's
own importer; bytes after zlib level 6 as the packager counts them; "colour / packed / normal" is the RDO loss for base
colour, for the AO-roughness-metal maps `*_arm_*` / `*_rough_*`, and for `*_nor_gl_*`):

| `.import` setting | Bytes | Crate close PSNR / SSIM | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `compress/mode=0` Lossless | 16.2 MB | reference | 4× the video memory of a VRAM format |
| `mode=2` BPTC (before) | 12.6 MB | 48.3 dB / 0.994 | |
| `mode=4` Basis UASTC, RDO 1 / 1 / 1 | 8.5 MB | 43.7 dB / 0.987 | |
| `mode=4`, RDO 2 / 2 / 2 | 7.5 MB | 40.8 dB / 0.973 | |
| `mode=4`, RDO 4 / 4 / 4 | 6.7 MB | 38.4 dB / 0.951 | wood grain visibly noisier at 0.35 m |
| `mode=4`, RDO 4 / 4 / 1 | 7.2 MB | 39.1 dB / 0.955 | normal maps carry little of the error |
| `mode=4`, RDO 1 / 4 / 1 | 7.8 MB | 43.2 dB / 0.985 | base colour carries most of it |
| **`mode=4`, RDO 1 / 4 / 4** | **7.4 MB** | **41.6 dB / 0.981** | chosen |

**Choice.** `compress/mode=4` (Basis Universal, Godot 4.7.2 encodes UASTC, supercompressed), `uastc_level=0`, mipmaps
on, `detect_3d/compress_to=0`, and the rate-distortion loss by role: base colour 1.0, packed maps 4.0, normal maps 4.0
(with `compress/normal_map=1`, transcoded to BC5 on desktop GPUs). The town body's maps take the same mode with base
colour 1.0, roughness and the hair opacity mask 1.0 (an alpha-to-coverage mask keeps the low loss), normal maps 4.0;
`hair_strands.png`, which `human.gd` loads but no tool owned, joins the table as base colour. No new dependency: this is
the engine's own encoder, as in `DEP-160`.

**No new downscale here.** The client's closest framing puts an eye 0.30–0.35 m from a wall, shelf or counter, where
a 1920-pixel-wide view at FOV 70° spans about 0.45 m (roughly 4 000 px/m), and 1.66 m above the floor, where the ground
under the feet is seen at about 800 px/m. The surfaces' tiling maps and the furniture's 1k maps are at or below those
densities. The per-prop exceptions, small goods whose 1k map packs more texels per metre than the camera resolves, are
measured by RL-b2's texel-density check and set by its per-slug `process/size_limit` table (8 slugs at 512 px, 13 kept at
1k by measurement); the C6 tree measured below already carries them. This record leaves `size_limit` alone.

**No dedup.** No two texture files under `assets/` are byte-identical (MD5 over all 251), so there is nothing to merge.

**Cast meshes: no change, with the reasons.**
- Godot's glTF importer turns vertex compression off for any primitive with `JOINTS_0` (and for morph targets), so
  `meshes/force_disable_compression` has no effect on the cast: measured byte-identical `.scn` either way. Forcing the
  flag on a skinned surface is a path the engine deliberately does not take, and is not adopted.
- `meshes/create_shadow_meshes=false`: no change in size (no separate shadow mesh is built for these bodies).
- `meshes/generate_lods=false` saves 17% of a body's scene (about 0.45 MB each, 10 MB for 22 bodies) but drops the LODs
  that 21 townspeople at 95 000 triangles each rely on at a distance. Not taken without a performance measurement.
- meshoptimizer / gltfpack (MIT) quantisation or `EXT_meshopt_compression` act on the glTF file; Godot imports either
  into its own float arrays, so the shipped `.scn` does not shrink. Simplification would, but it changes the silhouette
  the clipping gates judge and the face seen at 0.5 m. Not adopted; no dependency added.

**Measured result** (S23 packager, `assemble --target macos-universal --clients 2d,3d` on the C6 tree with the
`DEP-160` imports and this record's 229 `.import` files): `3d.pck` **364.8 → 288.3 MB** compressed (the 230 other
textures 211 → 135 MB); the archive **523.7 → 447.3 MB**. Both still exceed their hard limits (250 and 400).

**What is left, and why it needs a ruling.** The pack is now 135 MB of prop, surface and town-body textures, 74.6 MB of
cast base colours (2048², `DEP-160`), 72 MB of scenes (56 MB cast bodies) and 7 MB else; the archive adds about 159 MB,
most of it two macOS universal Godot runtimes (one per client, `D-Ra-1`). Reaching 250 / 400 needs at least one of:
cast base colours at 1024² (about −56 MB; `DEP-160` measured 41–43.5 dB at the face, against the Q-S26-5 ruling that
2048² stays), one shared Godot runtime for both clients (about −60 MB archive; an S23 packaging change), cast LODs off
(−10 MB, a performance cost), or cast mesh simplification (about −25 MB, re-gating clipping). Each changes a ruled
standard or a frozen design, so none is taken here.
