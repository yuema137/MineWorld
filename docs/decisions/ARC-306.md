## ARC-306 — Every piece of furniture a body could meet is a solid, in the place's data and in the slice

**Date** 2026-10-19 · **Status** proposed (in PR `fix/vf-furniture-solids`; the operator accepts it with
the PR) · **Relates to** `ARC-39`, `ARC-50`, `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-11-bodies.md` §19.3.1
(amended here), `.structured-coding/plans/mvp1/pr-vf-furniture-solids.md` (the design and the open
question), [`docs/VISUAL_FIDELITY.md`](../VISUAL_FIDELITY.md)

**Problem.** In the 3D client people walked through the café's chairs and stools. Step-11 §19.3.1 made a
place's solids the boxes the 3D slice *collides* with, and recorded "the stools and front board are
visual". The slice placed the six table chairs and the three window stools with `SliceProps.put`
(no collider) and the drinks ledge with `Build.box` (no collider), so neither the server
(`places/cafe.yaml`) nor the slice's own physics held them: the player's body, every NPC's
server-resolved route and every stride passed through them. The recorded run (`origin/main @370b9da0`,
`run-a/a-3d.avi` 20–23 s) shows the visitor's legs through a window stool; the server accepted the
visitor at (1 596, 1 139) on the ledge's line.

**Choice.**

1. A drawn object that stands on the floor and that a person's body could meet — a chair, a stool, a
   ledge at hip height — is a solid. "Visual only" is kept for what a body cannot meet: things on a
   table or counter, things on a wall, things overhead, rugs.
2. The solid is authored in the place's `body.solids` (the data the `bodies` System owns, `ARC-39`) and
   the slice draws the same object with a collider, sized as §19.3.1's `put_solid` rule sizes one (the
   asset's glTF bounds, yawed, × 0.92, centred where the slice places it), so the server and the
   client stop a body at the same place within 150 mm.
3. Both towns hold the same café (`town_bodies.rs` TD-3), so both get the ten new solids: six chairs,
   three stools, the ledge.

**Not decided here.** The slice still draws its furniture from positions written in GDScript, and the
world's solids are a hand transcription of them. A world whose café differs would be drawn with the
slice's furniture where its solids are not. Making one data source both the server's solids and the
client's drawn furniture is a contract question, asked in the design record above.

**Why.** It is the frozen route (`ARC-39`: solids are place data, the server resolves every stride) with
one content row corrected; no contract changes.
