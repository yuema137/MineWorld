## ARC-179 — A station is depiction data: the `stations` section, doors into rooms off a room, and sit points told apart

**Date** 2026-10-26 · **Status** accepted (S33-e frozen 2026-10-19; Q-S33e-1 ruled as recommended, splitting
S33-e2 off; the operator's binding rule for S33-e: "no world-specific code") · **Relates to** `ARC-177` (the
depiction file, extended here), `ARC-178` (decks, the precedent), `ARC-192` (the station kit and its pieces),
`ARC-108` (a fixture shows only what was disclosed), `ARC-307` (furniture is the world's), `ARC-145` (sit
points), `ARC-171` (nothing moves) · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s33e-pier-halt.md` §5.2 and
§15 (S33-e2) · **Specification** [`clients/3d-spike/PRESENTATION.md`](../../clients/3d-spike/PRESENTATION.md) §7
and §10, [`MODULE_SPEC.md`](../MODULE_SPEC.md) §6.1.2

**Problem.** Lakeside's halt is a small timber waiting room with a ticket bay north of it and a platform east of
it, one track, a buffer stop by the water, a shut window and a board that says there is no service. S33-e
revision 1 drew it in a Lakeside script (`halt.gd`), which the operator's rule forbids. Format 1 could draw the
waiting room's shell, but not three things the halt needs:

1. a door from one depicted place into another that opens off it (the waiting room into its ticket bay and onto
   its platform): an opening could stand only `at_doorway` of its own place, and only there did the shell leave
   a gap in its colliders;
2. S29-e's station pieces (board, clock, ticket window, platform, track) placed from data and wired to the
   disclosed state;
3. a way to tell the waiting room, tagged `[station]`, from the ticket bay and the platform, which carry
   `station` too: binding by "every tag present" takes all three.

**Choice.**

1. An opening may state **`doorway_of: [tag, ...]`** in place of `offset_m` or `at_doorway`: the door stands on
   the doorway of the place those tags name, may be `open`, and leaves its collider gap.
2. An **optional** section **`stations`** joins format 1 (every existing file stays valid). A station has a
   style (`timber` or `oak`, `StationPieces.style_of`), **fixtures** — a board, a clock or a ticket window bay —
   each `in` one place at a plan point and height, facing a bearing, and an optional **platform** that depicts a
   place, with its edge, its height above the rail head, coping, warning strip, end fences, an optional
   cantilevered canopy and one **track** (gauge, near distance, ties, rails, an optional buffer end, the run
   beyond the platform and a cutting). `DepictionStations` draws them with S29-e's `StationPieces` and
   `StationTrack`, unchanged, and registers every fixture with `StationFixtures` by the place's scene volume, so
   a fixture shows only the disclosed state of its place (`ARC-108`). The platform's slab is ground; the terrain
   is cut under it and along the trackbed; the woods keep off the trackbed.
3. **One place per tag list.** A `doorway_of`, a fixture's `in` and a platform's `depicts` name the place tagged
   exactly so, else the only place carrying every tag; anything else is `DEPICTION_REFERENCE`. A sit point's
   depiction may state **`unless: [tag, ...]`**: tags its place must not carry (the shared `MineWorldSitRule`,
   both clients).
4. `mineworld_packages::depiction_station` checks the section inside the existing stages (`MALFORMED` …
   `ON_FLOOR`: a window bay's fields; one place per tag list; ranges; a fixture on its place, the canopy along
   its platform, the trackbed on no place's floor). The client's reader knows the section's shape and the
   canopy's texture.
5. Nothing is drawn that the world does not have: no train, no signal aspect, no clerk; the board, the window
   and the clock follow the disclosure; nothing moves (`ARC-171`). The benches are the world's furnishings,
   drawn by S29-e's `bench_row` piece through a binding (`bench.platform`).

**Reuse.** S29-e's pieces and track builders, unchanged (`ARC-192`'s note: "functions of size, not of the
depot", R-S29e-e-1); the shell builder for the building; the deck's ground rule for the platform. The depot's
station kit (`stations/<kit>.yaml`) was not reused for the halt: it describes an arched depot (towers, arcade,
annex) by role, and a two-room timber halt fits it only by leaving most of it empty; the halt's building is an
ordinary shell. No dependency.

**Consequences.** Another world's halt — a waiting room, rooms off it, a platform, a line — is data only; the
generated variant `lakeside-v7` draws its halt from the same section with another style, canopy and line. A town
whose station is the depot kit keeps the kit.

**Revisit** when a platform serves more than one track, a line needs a signal or a level crossing, or a station
needs fixtures the pieces lack (a timetable case, a bell).
