## ARC-147 — The `posture` System Pack: sit points as place data, `posture-state` for the seated, `sit` and `stand` as complete offers, auto-stand on movement

**Date** 2026-10-18 · **Status** accepted for the frozen step S34 and its PR S34-a (`pr-s34a-posture-pack.md`), pending the operator's merge review · **Relates to** `ARC-25`, `ARC-26`, `ARC-29`, `ARC-31`, `ARC-33`, `ARC-34`, `ARC-39`, `ARC-61`, `ARC-63`, `ARC-65`, `ARC-91`, `ARC-145`; `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-34-posture.md` §§5, 6, 10, 12, 13, 16 (rulings Q-S34-1…14, 2026-10-18), §17

**Problem.** A walking world has people who stand where they stopped. Nothing says that a bench in a café is a place a person may sit, that one person may sit on it at a time, or that a seated person is still where they sat. Each client would invent a rule for it, and a 2D and a 3D client would disagree about who is on the bench. The step asks for one owner of that fact, with the answer served to every client and to every agent the same way (`INV-13`, `ARC-34`).

**Choice.**

1. **Sit points are data of their place, not entities** (Q-S34-2). A `sit-points:` section in a place file (`ARC-31`), owned by `posture`, seeded as the posture-owned genesis fact `place-sit-points` and reduced into the component `SitPoints` (type `sit-points`, schema 1) on the Place. Items are kinds with counts (`ARC-36`), a System may not create entities at run time (`QPL-11`), and a sit point needs no identity across places.
2. **A sit point is free floor** (Q-S34-3). Its `at` is the seated person's position on the floor in front of the seat; the pelvis over the seat is presentation. The server never states a height (`I-S34-8`).
3. **Sitting is a row on the Person, standing is its absence** (`SD-S34-5`). `posture-state`, schema 1, `Seated { point, since }`; a person who never sits holds no row, so a world without the pack is byte-identical (`I-S34-6`). The value is an enum so that lying or kneeling are later variants of this pack. The furniture term is **sit point**; the pack is `posture` (Q-S34-1); `posture-state` never reads as the appearance attribute `posture` of S30-e.
4. **Two actions, validated in one fixed order that the offer shares** (`I-a-1`). `sit { point }` answers, in order: `Unavailable` (world without the pack), `PreconditionFailed` (not a person, or no presence), `PermissionDenied` (the Interaction List forbids it), `NoSupportedInteraction` (no such point in the actor's place), `Busy` (already seated, or walking, Q-S34-6), `TooFarAway` (beyond `reach` from `at`), `TargetUnavailable` (another person seated there, or a standing person within 510 mm of `at`, or presence would refuse the arrival). `stand {}` answers `PreconditionFailed` for a non-person or a person who is not seated, and is never governed by the list. Refusals follow Q-S34-7.
5. **Seating moves the person through presence, not through posture.** On acceptance `sit` states presence's `arrived` through presence's constructor, unless the person already stands exactly there facing that way, and then its own `sat-down`; `stand` states `stood-up { cause: requested }` and the location does not change (`I-S34-3`).
6. **Standing up because the person moved** (Q-S34-4, auto-stand). A seated person whose new location is not their point's place and position is stood up in the same dispatch (`stood-up { cause: moved }`). A change of facing alone does not stand them up.
7. **Offers and disclosure** (`ARC-34`). A standing observer sees one complete `sit { point }` per sit point in key order, each refused for the reason dispatch would give; a seated observer sees one complete `stand {}`. `SitPoints` are disclosed about the place to whoever perceives it; `posture-state` about a person to whoever perceives them. Nothing is offered about another person.
8. **Biography** (`ARC-29`). Neither `sat-down` nor `stood-up` is biographical, and the Interaction List cannot make them so.
9. **The Interaction List section** (`ARC-63`, `ARC-65`; `configure/posture.yaml`, the carrier QIL-2 keeps). It governs `sit` for actor and place roles, one parameter `reach` (750 mm, 300 … 1 500, Q-S34-8), and the two facts' audiences (narrowing only). It cannot govern `stand`.
10. **Bodies' part is a later step.** Q-S34-5 (a seated person cannot be pushed) is a bodies change, `Anchoring`, and lands in S34-b under this record's second step; until then `posture` alone does not stop a walker from moving a seated person, which the auto-stand of (6) answers honestly.

**Rulings** (`step-34-posture.md` §16, 2026-10-18, as recommended except where stated): Q-S34-1 names; Q-S34-2 sit points are data; Q-S34-3 free floor; Q-S34-4 auto-stand (operator's ruling); Q-S34-5 a seated person cannot be pushed (bodies, S34-b); Q-S34-6 `sit` while walking is `Busy`; Q-S34-7 the refusal mapping; Q-S34-8 reach 750 mm and spacing 510 mm; Q-S34-9 posture lands before S33-f; Q-S34-10 the 2D seated view comes from S32's route; Q-S34-11 a 60 s gate is measured first, not built; Q-S34-12 procedural transitions; Q-S34-13 sitting only in MVP-0; Q-S34-14 implementers.

**Consequences.** The pack's crate is `systems/posture` (`mineworld-posture`), installed by one line in `systems/installed`. Its dependencies are contracts, kernel, authoring, sdk, presence and movement; nothing depends on it. No shipped world names `posture` in its `systems:` list in this record's PR, so every shipped world stays byte-identical. A client sees sit points as place data and seated people as `posture-state` in observations; a client never computes distance, stillness or occupancy (`I-S34-4`). The sit requirement is declared through the offer; a world's list changes the verdict, never the rule.

**Bounded corrections at implementation** (S34-a, 2026-10-18/19; `pr-s34a-posture-pack.md` §11). (a) BC-5: `sit`'s offered requirement is `at_place(<the observer's place>)`, not `same_place()`, because `SamePlaceAsActor` needs a target entity and a sit point is not one; reach, occupancy and walking travel as the offer's refusal, so the offer and dispatch still answer alike. (b) BC-6: the row is `Seated { place, point, since }`. A key is unique only within its place, and presence has already written the new location when posture reacts to `arrived`, so the row is the only record of the place sat in; I-S34-2 makes it always equal to the Presence place while the row exists. Auto-stand also reacts to movement's `walk-started`, so a seated person who sets off on a walk stands up at once rather than at the first stride. (c) BC-7 is void: main's bodies now states `PERSON_RADIUS` = 250 mm and `GAP` = 10 mm (12d), so the 510 mm spacing is `2R + GAP` as ruled. (d) BC-10: the place-file section key is `sit-points:` (an authored section name is `a`–`z`, `0`–`9` and `-`); everywhere this record or the step writes `sit_points:` it means `sit-points:`.

**Revisit** when S34-b adds bodies' `Anchoring` catalog (the record's second step), when S34-c enables the pack in the shipped worlds (a recorded re-baseline of their genesis), or when a lying or kneeling posture is added (a schema change of this pack, not of the kernel).
