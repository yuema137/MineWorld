## ARC-284 — Needs are lazy deficits owned by the `needs` System Pack, disclosed to their holder only, in words the pack owns, with facts at genesis only

**Date** 2026-10-18 · **Status** accepted (design, step 35); to be implemented by PR N-b · **Approved by** the operator, in the primary session, 2026-10-18 (step 35 frozen; Q-S35-2 and Q-S35-3 ruled: hunger and tiredness only, motivation only; Q-S35-4, Q-S35-5, Q-S35-11, Q-S35-12 as recommended; the generic-mechanism ruling below) · **Relates to** `CLAUDE.md` §4 rules 1, 2, 10, 11; `INV-13`, `ARC-25`, `ARC-28`, `ARC-31`, `ARC-62`, `ARC-63`, `ARC-86` (the lazy-decay precedent), `ARC-283`, `ARC-285`, `ARC-286`, `DEP-48` · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-35-needs-and-sleep.md` §§5, 6.1, 7; supersedes step-24 §6.5 and QS24-8

**Problem.** Nobody in a MineWorld world is ever hungry or tired (F-S35-1). `consumption` offers `eat` and `drink` but says itself that "nobody gets hungry". A need is a fact about a person — Alice *is* hungry — that a controller, an LM prompt, an MCP agent and two HUDs must all agree on. So it cannot live in a controller, and no renderer may compute it. It must also not grow the event log: 12 people whose hunger changed every minute would write millions of facts a year.

**Options considered.**

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| (a) Needs in each controller, as `motivated`'s hour curves already are | Declined: two controllers would disagree about one person, and an agent or a HUD could not see them |
| (b) A Process that raises every need on a timer and writes a fact each step | Declined for the reason `ARC-86` declined it for forgetting: facts, journal rows and save growth every day |
| (c) A threshold fact on a timer ("Alice became hungry") | Declined: still a fact per crossing per person, and the words would be fixed in the log rather than read from the current level |
| (d) **A `needs` System Pack with stored levels whose current value is computed where it is read** | **Chosen** |

**Choice.**

- **Which needs.** Two in MVP-0: hunger and tiredness (Q-S35-2). Thirst, hygiene and comfort are MVP-1.
- **Deficits.** Each need is an integer deficit in 0 … 1 000: 0 is sated or fully rested, 1 000 famished or exhausted. `Needs { hunger, tiredness }` stores each as `(level, at, mode)`, `mode` being `awake` or `asleep`.
- **Lazily.** The current level is a pure function, `clamp(level + rate(need, mode) × (now − at) ÷ 3 600, 0, 1 000)`, computed wherever it is read: at disclosure, in a reaction. A reaction first folds the levels to its fact's instant, applies the fact, and stores `(new level, fact instant, new mode)`. Integer arithmetic only.
- **What changes a need.** Reactions to other packs' facts, decoded through their owners' types (`ARC-28`): inventory's `items-consumed` lowers hunger by the item's `nourishment.hunger` × count (`ARC-285`); sleep's `fell-asleep` and `woke` switch the mode (`ARC-283`). Which restorers a world uses is a closed enum in the pack's section, `restore: { hunger: [eating], tiredness: [sleeping] }` by default. A restorer whose pack the world does not enable is refused by name at load (`eating` needs `consumption`, `sleeping` needs `sleep`), so a need never silently stops falling. A third restorer is when an `ARC-62` catalog earns its place (Q-S35-4); two do not.
- **Who has needs.** Only a person whose file carries a `needs:` section (`needs: {}` takes the defaults, hunger 300 and tiredness 700, the levels of a person at midnight), as `holdings:` works (Q-S35-5). A person without one — a statue, a ghost — has none.
- **Facts.** `needs-assigned { person, levels }` at genesis, one per person with the section. Nothing after: decay writes zero facts, zero journal rows and starts no Process.
- **Disclosure.** `Needs { hunger, tiredness, words }` to the holder only (`INV-13`). A stranger cannot see that Alice is hungry.
- **Words owned by the pack.** The pack maps a level to a word with ordered thresholds (default 400 / 600 / 900): hunger `ok`, `peckish`, `hungry`, `famished`; tiredness `ok`, `sleepy`, `tired`, `exhausted`. Every reader uses the disclosed word. No client, controller or prompt computes it.
- **Numbers are data.** Rates and thresholds are typed, bounded Interaction List parameters in `configure/needs.yaml`, scoped by class like every parameter (`{ actor: night-shift, … }`). Defaults, from the committed ATUS A-3 2025 table (`DEP-48`) and the AASM/SRS consensus, are fixed before any run (`ARC-23`): tiredness +60/h awake, rest −125/h asleep, hunger +120/h awake and +30/h asleep (authored). `MODULE_SPEC.md` §4.2 lists their bounds.
- **Motivation only** (Q-S35-3). A famished or exhausted person suffers no forced consequence in MVP-0. A forced collapse would make `sleep` read `needs` and couple the two packs; consequences are later rules of other packs in the Interaction List.

**The generic-mechanism ruling (operator, 2026-10-18).** The mechanism is generic and data-driven. Nothing in `needs` names a world, a person or an item: initial levels are in person files, nourishment in item files, rates and thresholds in the world's section. Market Town's food supply and prices (F-S35-3, Q-S35-8) are content in N-d, not code. Every acceptance criterion holds on a second world or a generated variant with no code change.

**Consequences.** Replay and kill/resume reproduce every value, because each is a function of stored state and world time (`ARC-25`). A world without `needs` is byte-identical to the base. A `needs:` section in a world that does not enable `needs` is refused by name (`ARC-31`). Individual variation from S30's `chronotype` and `sleep-need` is later work in the owning pack's own PR (Q-S35-11).

**Revisit** when a third restorer appears (a catalog), when a need must have a consequence, or when S30's attributes are read.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (PR N-b; SD-Nb-1, SD-Nb-3, SD-Nb-6, F-Nb-3, F-Nb-4) — as implemented.** The `rest_per_hour`
default's secondary source was re-verified at N-b's C0: Watson N. F. et al., "Recommended Amount of Sleep for a
Healthy Adult: A Joint Consensus Statement of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research
Society", *Sleep* 38(6):843–844, 2015, doi:10.5665/sleep.4716 (<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979105/>):
"Adults should sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis to promote optimal health." Besides
`needs-assigned`, the pack states two genesis facts nobody perceives: `nourishment-assigned { item, hunger }`
(authored content becomes state only as a genesis fact, `ARC-15`) and its configured fact; still nothing after
genesis and no Process. Each fold truncates toward zero (at most one unit lost per fold; folds happen only at
a meal, falling asleep or waking). A fact that changes nothing — a meal of a kind that nourishes 0, a fact
about a person without needs — writes nothing. The rates are looked up with the person as `actor` and no
place, so a person's needs move at the same rates wherever they are. The disclosed `needs` record carries,
besides the levels and words, `asleep`, the `rates` and `restore` in force, and `nourishment`: each kind the
holder carries that has the item form, with its amount — so a controller picks the most nourishing food and
sizes a sleep without reading an item file or hard-coding a rate (`MODULE_SPEC.md` §4.2). The thresholds are
not disclosed.
