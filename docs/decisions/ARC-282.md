## ARC-282 — Departure is each controller's decision: `Consult.stride`, the shared estimate, `rule`'s policy data, `motivated`'s scored option, the LM seat's `go`

**Date** 2026-10-18 · **Status** accepted (operator rulings R-LE2-1, R-LE2-2, 2026-10-18; Q-LE2-1 … Q-LE2-2, Q-LE-4 … Q-LE-6 as recommended) · **Approved by** the operator, in the primary session · **Relates to** `ARC-27` (models are pure), `ARC-84` (decision models; `Consult`), `ARC-63`, `ARC-280`, `ARC-281`; amends P6 I-P6-3, step-17 I-15 and QP6-3 (a) narrowly (an LM seat may choose to set out for a place) · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-le-leave-early.md` SD-LE-4, SD-LE-5, §0.4

**Problem.** Revision 1 of PR LE put one departure rule, with code constants, in a module both in-process models obeyed. The operator ruled that leaving early must not be one hard-coded behaviour: each behaviour model has its own mechanism, an LLM-driven person decides from the rough travel time and the start of work, and the mechanisms must be generic and data-driven so the next world works too.

**Choice.**

1. **The world discloses facts only** (`ARC-280`, `ARC-281`); the host states its stride cadence: `mineworld_decision::Consult` gains `stride: SimDuration`, *how often the host asks for the next stride of a walk, in world seconds* — `RUN_STEP` (30 s) in `run`, the seat's step (`scale` s) hosted. Host context like `pace`; a model that ignores it decides as before.
2. **The shared arithmetic** (`mineworld_rule_controller::commute`, pure, integer): `strides = ceil(length ÷ WALK_STRIDE) + doors + 1`; `estimate = strides × stride`; `lead = min(cap, estimate × (1 000 + slack) ÷ 1 000 + margin)`, the cap when the place is not in `ways`; due at `t` ⇔ the next part is punctual ∧ I am not there ∧ `t + pace > next.from − lead` ∧ `t < next.from`. The Python twin (`mineworld_sdk.ways.estimate`) is LE-c's and is checked against the same literal table.
3. **`rule` — a policy, as data.** `Rule`'s parameters become `RuleParameters { commute }`, decoded with positioned refusals, written under `models: { rule: { parameters: { commute: … } } }` (the decision list's model-entry shape; §0.4's sketch omits the `parameters:` level): `mode` (`plan` | `at-boundary`), `slack` ‰ 0 … 2 000 (500), `margin_minutes` 0 … 60 (5), `cap_minutes` 15 … 480 (120), `precedence` (`first` | `after-replies`). A due commute asks `walk-to` the next part's place with no draw; once the walk goes there, only a line may be answered between strides and nothing redirects the walk. `at-boundary` restores the old lateness as data. A person already at a punctual next part's place within the cap and one pace of its `from` waits there (`commute::holds`): the part in force no longer calls them away; earlier, it has its day as usual. `motivated` applies the same rule.
4. **`motivated` — a scored option, no hard rung.** `Choice::Commute(place)` while a punctual next part is ahead and elsewhere; its score is `base.commute` (1 000) × `departure(urgency)`, urgency 0 until two paces before the leave-by instant, 1 000 from it, linear between; default `departure` 0 → 0, 500 → 700, 1 000 → 1 000. While the urgency is above 0, `unhurried` (0 → 1 000, 500 → 300, 1 000 → 0) scales the reply and invitation thresholds and the greet, approach, invite and wander scores, and neither the agenda in force nor a doorway is offered. The `commute` block is `rule`'s; the curves are overridable. No new draw.
5. **LM seats** decide from the facts in their prompt and may answer `go` to a place (LE-c); MCP agents see the same facts (LE-c). Nothing sets out for an LM seat while the model holds it.

**Consequences.** No departure rule lives in the world, the kernel, the SDK or a client (I-LE2-1). In a world with no punctual part every decision of `rule` and `motivated` is what it was (I-LE-4): `due` and `Pending` need a punctual next part, and `unhurried` joins a score only while a commute presses. The defaults were fixed before any run (`ARC-23`); `motivated`'s on-time target (≥ 900 ‰) is recorded at the milestone, never tuned. A `PacedRuleController` built with `new` plans no commute; `Rule` builds it with the seat's policy and the host's stride.

**Revisit** when S30's `core.punctuality` attribute lands (F-LE-3): it could scale a person's margin.

### Amendment 2026-10-26 (F-Nh-5)

The hold window of item 3 (`commute::holds`) is no longer "within the cap and one pace of `from`". A person at a punctual next part's place is held when `t + pace > from − window`, where `window = min(cap, estimate(way back) + lead(way back))` and the way back is the disclosed way to the place of the part in force; the cap when no such way is disclosed or that part is here. A commute's own arrival always falls inside the window, so a commuter waits as before. A person who arrived early for another reason (a fetch errand at a shop that is also their workplace, whose food sold out before they reached the counter) is no longer held for up to two hours and goes back while a round trip still fits. `motivated` applies the same rule. Evidence and trace: `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s35-nh-hands-and-errands.md` §6.2, F-Nh-5.
