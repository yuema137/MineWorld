## ARC-178 — A deck on piles is depiction data: the `decks` section of format 1

**Date** 2026-10-21 · **Status** accepted (S33-e frozen 2026-10-19; Q-S33e-1 and Q-S33e-2 ruled as recommended,
relayed 2026-10-21; the operator's binding rule for S33-e1: "no world-specific code") · **Relates to** `ARC-177`
(the depiction file, extended here), `ARC-307` (furniture is the world's), `ARC-170` (Lakeside's heights),
`ARC-171` · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s33e-pier-halt.md` §5.1.1; S33-d revision 2's
requirement R-S33e-d-2 · **Specification** [`clients/3d-spike/PRESENTATION.md`](../../clients/3d-spike/PRESENTATION.md)
§6, [`MODULE_SPEC.md`](../MODULE_SPEC.md) §6.1.2

**Problem.** Lakeside's landmark is a fishing pier: a deck on piles over the lake, a ramp down from the lane,
rails, an open pavilion and moored boats. Format 1 (`ARC-177`) cannot say "a walked floor raised on piles", and
S33-e's revision-1 plan drew the pier in a Lakeside script, which the operator's rule forbids: another world's
jetty would need another script.

**Choice.**

1. An **optional** section `decks` joins format 1; every existing file stays valid. A deck depicts a place by its
   tags; its `footprint_mm` is that place's floor (within 50 mm); `surface_m` is where its people stand. Fields:
   boards, an optional `approach` ramp (no steeper than 1:12, 2010 ADA Standards §405.2), piles in bents with
   optional X-bracing, optional rails on named sides, an optional edge curb, an optional open `shelter` (posts
   inside the deck, a roof of the building roof type, an optional lantern on a post), and static `moored` craft
   that may not stand on any place's floor. Every deck is cited.
2. `mineworld_packages::depiction_deck` checks it inside the existing stages, so the code order is unchanged
   (`MALFORMED` … `ON_FLOOR`). The client's reader knows the section's shape and its texture slugs.
3. One generic builder, `DepictionDecks` (`clients/3d-spike/scripts/depictions/data/deck_builder.gd`), draws any
   deck; it names no world, place key or tag. Three generic rules follow from "the deck is the floor":
   the depicted place's floor height is the deck's surface (its door's in-point, its frame origin, the server's
   solids and its furnishings stand there); the terrain stays below a deck's underside; a drawn person stands on
   the depiction's ground colliders (terrain and decks) under its observed point.
4. Furniture on a deck stays the world's `body: furnishings` (`ARC-307`): the section has no field for a bench.

**Reuse.** Ours, from `Build` boxes and cylinders and the shell builder's roof (the town's one method, S29 §6.1;
S33-e §4). No dependency. The reuse candidates `ARC-177` rejected for shells (CSG, func_godot, Cyclops) are
rejected here for the same reason: a deck must be driven by a file the Rust validator also reads.

**Consequences.** Another world's pier, jetty or boardwalk is data only; S33-e2's platform may be a deck. The
generated variant `lakeside-v7` keeps the pier (its place is the world's) and is drawn by the same builder.

**Revisit** when a deck needs a non-rectangular footprint, steps, or a floating (moving) pontoon, which would need
a disclosed `Process` (`ARC-171`).
