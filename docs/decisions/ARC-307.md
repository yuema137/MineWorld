## ARC-307 — The furniture both clients draw comes from the world's data: furnishings, named by their solids

**Date** 2026-10-19 · **Status** accepted (design frozen by the operator 2026-10-19, Q-FD-1…8 as
recommended; implemented in PR `mvp1/pr-vf-furnishings`) · **Relates to** `ARC-39` (amended), `ARC-306`
(completed), `ARC-46`, `ARC-147`, [`CORE_CONCEPTS.md`](../CORE_CONCEPTS.md) §6.5,
[`MODULE_SPEC.md`](../MODULE_SPEC.md) `body:`,
`.structured-coding/plans/mvp1/pr-vf-furnishings-from-data.md` (the design)

**Problem.** The café's tables, chairs and stools were written twice: once as positions in the 3D
client's GDScript, and once, by hand transcription, as solids in both towns' `places/cafe.yaml`. A
world whose café differs would be drawn with the slice's furniture where its solids are not. Four more
free-standing pieces — the display shelves, the east shelf unit and two potted plants — were drawn with
no solid at all, so a body walked into them (`ARC-306` residue). The 2D client placed its café tables
and plants by hand, too.

**Choice.**

1. A place's `body:` may list **furnishings**: `{ key, kind, at, facing }` — what stands where, and what
   it is. A solid may name the furnishing it belongs to (`furnishing: <key>`). One piece may have
   several solids.
2. The link is total, held by the type (`PlaceShape::checked`): every furnishing is named by at least
   one solid, every named key is listed, each `at` lies on the floor and inside its own solids, keys are
   unique, at most 64 furnishings, `facing` 0 … 359 999. A piece with nothing a body can meet cannot be
   written (`ARC-306` by construction).
3. Furnishings travel inside `place-shape`, which stays **schema 1**: the field is serialized only when
   present, so every saved fact and snapshot decodes, and encodes, exactly as before.
4. The look is a Presentation Pack's. The 3D pack binds a kind to a model of the client's library
   (`presentation/mineworld-default/3D/furniture/furnishings.yaml`), the 2D pack binds the role
   `furnishing.<kind>`; both fall back by `.`-prefix (`chair.painted` → `chair`) and, with nothing bound,
   draw plain boxes over the piece's solids and report it. No client script names a place, a world or a
   kind; no client places a piece in code. The standalone 3D slice draws from a recorded,
   drift-checked observation.
5. Free-standing pieces are furnishings; the building's own parts (walls, a fitted counter, a built-in
   bench, a dresser, a door leaf) are shell: plain solids, drawn by the client's depiction and checked
   by the geometry probe.

**Amends `ARC-39`.** A place's solids may now carry a furnishing key, and a place's body may carry
furnishings; the resolver reads neither.

**Not decided here.** Disclosure stays the observer's own place, so the street's furniture and the
florist's interior (seen through windows before entered) keep their client-placed drawing until a later
design widens disclosure of static place data (Q-FD-3). A furnishing that moves, is bought or carried
would be an Item with a body (`ARC-36`), not a furnishing.

**Why.** One data source is what both the server resolves against and what both clients draw, so a
second world, or a moved table, needs no client change; the link from a drawn piece to its solids is
the data S36-P5's mesh-against-solid check needs. Items (option (c)) are heavy for things that never
move; a `kind` on each solid cannot express a piece of two boxes.
