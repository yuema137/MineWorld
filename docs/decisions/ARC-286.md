## ARC-286 — Controllers and needs: inert when absent; precedence asleep → due commute → eat → social; each controller's needs behaviour is its own policy

**Date** 2026-10-18 · **Status** accepted (design, step 35, as amended by PR LE revision 2; amended 2026-10-19 by Q-S35-OP1, below); to be implemented by PR N-c (in-process models) and N-e (agent and LM views) · **Approved by** the operator, in the primary session, 2026-10-18 (step 35 frozen; the LE rulings R-LE2-1 and R-LE2-2, applied to needs by the coordinator; the generic-mechanism ruling below) · **Relates to** `CLAUDE.md` §1 (models removable), §4 rules 3, 10; `INV-1`, `ARC-27`, `ARC-84`, `ARC-280`, `ARC-282`, `ARC-283`, `ARC-284`, `DEP-47`, `DEP-48`, `MODULE_SPEC.md` §5.1 · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/step-35-needs-and-sleep.md` §6; `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-le-leave-early.md` §0.4

**Problem.** With `sleep` and `needs` in a world, every kind of controller must use them: `rule`, `motivated`, an LM seat, an MCP agent and a human. Three things can go wrong. A controller can change behaviour in a world that never enabled the packs, which breaks every reference world's baseline. A sleeper can stay in bed through a shift, or a hungry person can stop to chat instead of eating. And the step's first draft put the needs thresholds (eat at 600, bed at 600 in the night window 22:00 – 06:00, at 900 at any hour) into `rule` as code constants. The operator's 2026-10-18 rulings on leaving early (`pr-le-leave-early.md` §0.1) forbid exactly that shape: the world discloses facts, and each controller kind decides by its own model.

**Options considered.**

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| (a) One shared "needs behaviour" every controller obeys | Declined by R-LE2-1: a hidden rule would decide for an LM seat |
| (b) Thresholds and the night window as constants in `rule` | Declined by R-LE2-2: tuned to one town, and a world author could not change them |
| (c) **The world discloses the needs and their words; each controller decides with its own policy, data in the decision list** | **Chosen** |

**Choice.**

1. **Inert when absent.** Every new band, consideration and prompt line is inert when `Needs` (and, for sleep, `Asleep` and the `sleep` offer) is not disclosed. It takes no draw and changes no precedence, so a world without the packs decides exactly as before (F-S35-4; AC-S35-2).
2. **Precedence, for the in-process models.** asleep → due commute → eat → social. A sleeper asks nothing except `wake` when a punctual next part's departure is due (the departure is each controller's own decision, `ARC-282`), and answers no line. Then a due commute. Then eating when hungry with food held. Then the social bands. `greet`, `approach` and `invite` skip a person disclosed `Asleep`.
3. **`rule`: policy data.** The eat threshold, the bedtime thresholds, the night window, the bedtime spread and how long to sleep for are typed parameters of `rule` in `decisions.yaml` under `models: { rule: … }`, decoded with positioned refusals like the commute policy of `ARC-282`. The step's numbers are the defaults (eat at hunger ≥ 600 the held kind with the highest `nourishment`, ties to the lowest `ItemId`; bed at tiredness ≥ 600 within 22:00 – 06:00 from ATUS *Sleeping* ≥ 471 ‰, or ≥ 900 at any hour; `for` = the least of `longest_minutes`, minutes to rested, and minutes to the next punctual part's departure; bedtime spread `mix(seed, observer) mod 60` minutes, draw index 16, the only draw). A world with no decision list gets them. `rule` does not shop for food; it relies on the offered band. The field names are fixed in N-c's PR design.
4. **`motivated`: scored options.** New considerations `hungry`, `sated`, `tired` and `wakeful` over the disclosed levels, each a curve parameter (default, overridable), each neutral (1 000) when `Needs` is absent (I-DLb-4). Sleep and eating compete in the existing band draw like any option. The hour curves `rested` and `hungry hour` stay: the hour says when people usually do it, the need says whether this person needs it now.
5. **LM seats.** The prompt carries the holder's needs in the owner's words ("I am hungry; I am sleepy") within its byte budget, and the offers include `sleep`, `wake`, `eat` and `buy`. The model decides. No hidden auto-sleep and no auto-eat while the model holds the seat. The fallback (`rule` with its policy) drives the seat whenever it is handed back.
6. **MCP agents and humans.** The `me` view (`NeedsLine`, `AsleepLine`) and the HUDs show the same disclosed words and offers. An agent is told nothing a human is not, and its requests are validated as anyone's.

**The generic-mechanism ruling (operator, 2026-10-18).** No controller code names a world, a place, a person or an hour tuned to Market Town. Every behaviour above is a disclosed fact, a typed parameter or a scored option. Every acceptance criterion holds on Market Town and on a second world or a generated variant (for example LE's `commute-town`, with homes at different distances and shifts at other hours) with no code change. Acceptance runs and mutations run at the milestone (SPEED FIRST).

**Consequences.** A world author changes a town's bedtimes or appetite without code: a `rule` parameter block or a `motivated` curve. `rule`'s eat threshold is the controller's own choice, so it may differ from the pack's word thresholds; the words stay the pack's. Tests use deterministic controllers and recorded cassettes; no live model (`CLAUDE.md` §4 rule 10).

**Revisit** when a needs behaviour cannot be expressed as a parameter or a scored option, or when S30's attributes vary bedtimes per person.

**Dated note, 2026-10-19 (S35 N-e C5; operator ruling Q-Ne-1, yes).** Choice 5 is amended narrowly, as `ARC-282` amended the `go` choice. An LM seat (1) reads its needs' words and its own sleep in its prompt, and a person it sees asleep is marked as asleep; (2) is woken to think by a `feeling` trigger, which fires when one of its need words changes and once on waking, never while it sleeps; and (3) may choose the `sleep` act with `minutes`, only where sleep offers `sleep` now. The request is built through `mineworld_sdk.offers` as `sleep { for: minutes }`. The world validates the bound and the place, and a refusal says why. No seat sleeps or eats without its model's choice; the `rule` fallback is unchanged, and a world without the packs renders and decides as before. ### Amendment 2026-10-19 (Q-S35-OP1)

**Ruling (operator, 2026-10-19, relayed by the coordinator), quoted.** "YES, as recommended. Add the small generic `rule` follow-up: (a) bed only in the night window (or the any-hour exhausted override raised to 1000) and sleep runs to the end of the window; (b) a policy-data band 'hungry with no food held → walk to a disclosed shop'. No need-rate or acceptance-threshold changes. Must stay world-agnostic (no Market Town constants); verify on Market Town AND commute-town, then re-run AC-S35-8/9 and push N-d. Record the ruling in the step ledger and a decision record."

**Why.** PR N-d's material stop S-Nd-1 (`pr-s35-nd-content.md`, its ledger): with the step's defaults, Market Town's 03:00 asleep share was 224–342 ‰ and its longest famished stretch up to 710 h, Commute Town's 14:00 asleep share ~600 ‰ (finding F-Nc-8). Two causes, both in `rule`'s policy: the "≥ 900 at any hour" bed trigger sent people to bed at 18:00–21:00 and "minutes to rested" woke them at 01:00–04:00; and `rule` never went for food, so a person whose day passed no shop ate once a month.

**Change, to point 3 of the choice (PR N-c2).** Policy data in `rule`'s `needs` block, no world named, everything inert when `Needs` is not disclosed and no draw taken:

1. `exhausted_from` defaults to 1 000 (was 900): outside the night only the fully exhausted go to bed — the ruling's alternative wording, chosen because a person who rises at 04:30 for an early shift is fully exhausted by the late afternoon at the step's rates, and a strict "never outside the night" would leave that case to no rule at all.
2. `sleep_until: night-end | rested`, default `night-end`: `for` is the least of the world's longest, the minutes to the **next** end of the night (`night.until`), and the commute's leave-by — so a sleep begun in the night, or by exhaustion just before it, runs to the night's end instead of waking a rested person at 03:00. `rested` keeps the step's first rule.
3. A fetch band after bed and before the agenda, `fetch_food` (default true), `fetch_from` (default 600, 1 … 1 000) and `shop_tags` (default `[shop]`, at most 8): at hunger ≥ `fetch_from` with nothing nourishing held (the `needs` record's `nourishment` list), buy an available offered `buy` of a kind not already held (lowest `ItemId`; a held kind that does not nourish is known not to help); in a place that sells (a `shop_tags` tag on the place, or any `buy` offered), nothing more; else walk to a doorway whose disclosed `to_tags` carry a `shop_tags` tag (lowest `PlaceId`); else, in a place with exactly one doorway, walk out through it. Every input is a disclosure (the observer's own `needs` and `holdings`, the offers, the place's tags and its passages' `to_tags`); a world whose shops carry another tag names it in `shop_tags`.

The statement "`rule` does not shop for food; it relies on the offered band" in point 3 above is superseded. The step's thresholds and `needs`' rates are unchanged (I-S35-7).

**`motivated` (finding, not changed).** `motivated` computes `for` with the same `sleep_minutes` ("minutes to rested") and has no night window of its own — its night is the ATUS `rest` curve. Giving it night-end sleep needs a new `motivated` parameter (the night's end) and a scored-option question, not the same small change; it is recorded as F-Nc2-1 for the milestone, with AC-DLb-4 still FAIL as before.

**Verified** on a test copy of Market Town and on `commute-town`, with no code change between them (`tools/cli/tests/needs_controllers.rs`, `rule_people_sleep_through_the_night_and_go_for_food_in_two_worlds`), and by N-d's re-run of AC-S35-8 and AC-S35-9.

**Bounded additions found by the re-run (PR N-c2, D-Nc2-4 and D-Nc2-5; recorded, not ruled).** (4) `bed_clear_of_doors_mm` (default 1 500 mm, 0 = off): at bedtime within that distance of a doorway, a person first walks to their own seeded spot on the floor `bodies` discloses, clear of every doorway — without it every sleeper of a one-door home lay across the way out, and early risers' walks stalled. (5) A sleeper is woken when the commute falls due within two paces, not one, since waking takes a consult; and the fetch band is skipped while the commute policy holds a person at a punctual part's place.
