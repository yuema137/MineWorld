## ARC-330 — Doorways stay passable

**Date** 2026-10-19 · **Approved by** the operator at the S35 N-f design freeze (Q-Nf-1 … Q-Nf-6 as
recommended) · **Amends** `ARC-75` (one `Wayfinder` method), `ARC-39` (a give-way candidate before a
stride degrades) · **Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s35-nf-doorways.md`

**Problem.** In a room with one doorway, idle people collect on the threshold: a walk to a place as a
whole ended on entering, on the doorway point, and the next arrival was nudged onto the first. A
walker leaving was then stopped by a person a nudge cannot move (the wall is behind them), stalled,
and the re-plan round that person found no way (`no-route`). Controllers retried every consult with
the same result (F-Nd-5, F-Nd-6). A sleeper nudged by a passer-by was woken as `acted` (F-Nc-7 (2)).
Any world with a one-door room and a few visitors hits this; it is not Market Town's data.

**Options considered.**

```text
ORCA / RVO2 (Apache-2.0), dodgy_2d (MIT/Apache-2.0)   needs every agent's velocity at one common tick;
                                                      f32; no determinism statement; does not move a
                                                      person standing still; deadlocks in bottlenecks
social force (Helbing–Molnár; JuPedSim, Vadere)       continuous float integration of all pedestrians;
                                                      built to measure clogging, not avoid it
WHCA* / doorway reservation                           blockers are not moving; needs time-indexed plans
avoidance priority (Unity, Detour crowd)              adapted as one rule: a walker outranks a person
                                                      who is not walking
RTS "moving units push idle units aside"              adopted (R2)
arrival spots off the threshold (The Sims' slots)     adopted (R1)
```

No crate is added: both adopted models extend code bodies already owns (its nudge and its lattice
search), and the rejected crates fail MineWorld's fixed constraints — integer positions in the log,
byte-identical replay (`ARC-25`), one request resolved at a time with no common tick (step-11 §21.3).

**Decision.**

1. **R1 — a walk to a place ends clear of the threshold.** Movement's `Wayfinder` gains
   `arrival_spot(world, person, place, entered_at) -> Option<LocalPosition>`, default `None`. After
   the crossing into a destination place that has no position, the walk plans one more leg to that
   spot and ends there. Bodies answers with the first point of the 50 mm lattice, in entry's E3 order
   from `entered_at`, that is free by the route planner's own test, `THRESHOLD_CLEAR` from every
   doorway point of the place, and two radii and a gap from everyone else standing there. Without a
   shape, or with no such point, the answer is `None` and the walk ends on entering, as before. That
   last leg is a courtesy: it never ends a walk `stalled` or `no-route`; it ends `arrived` where the
   walker got to.
2. **R2 — a person who is not walking gives way.** When a walker's stride (one with a walk in
   progress; a direct `move` outranks nobody) falls short of its target because people who have no
   `Walking` (movement's component, read by type) stand in its corridor, bodies moves them, nearest
   the walker first, each to the nearest free lattice point outside the corridor within
   `GIVE_WAY_MAX` of them — `THRESHOLD_CLEAR` from every doorway point when such a point exists, else
   the nearest one off the corridor — reached by a segment that crosses no solid (swept against the
   fixed geometry only, like a nudge); at most `2 × NUDGED_MAX` of them. The result is verified
   (V1–V4) against everyone like every candidate; otherwise the stride stands as resolved. A walker
   never gives way by this rule, so two walkers still meet with the head-on bias (SD-B10).
3. **R3 — a re-plan round the blocker falls back once to the plain plan.** When the re-plan with
   `avoid` answers `Unreachable`, movement plans once more without it before ending the walk
   `no-route`; the next stride meets the blocker, and R2 moves them.
4. **R4 — another person's walk does not wake a sleeper.** Presence states the `arrived` of a person
   moved aside by another's arrival with both as participants (`[moved, mover]`). Sleep wakes a
   sleeper as `acted` only for an `arrived` whose participants are the sleeper alone. Bodies also
   states `gave-way { person, for }` for each such arrival, so clients can show a step aside.
5. **Constants** of the pack, not world data: `THRESHOLD_CLEAR` = `GIVE_WAY_MAX` = 1 200 mm — one
   person standing (R 250) plus a passing lane of one person's width plus the gap; the ADA 2010
   §404.2.3 minimum clear door width of 815 mm plus a radius each side; Fruin (1971) level-of-service
   A, about 1.2 m² per standing pedestrian.

**Deviations from the frozen design, recorded at implementation.**
- `arrival_spot` also takes the walker (`person`), so that the spot keeps clear of everyone else
  standing in the place; without it every arrival would aim at the same point.
- R4's pairing cannot be read from a `gave-way` fact: the kernel reduces facts generation by generation
  (`BD-7`), so sleep hears the moved sleeper's `arrived` before any reaction to it exists. The mark
  is therefore on the `arrived` itself, as its participants, stated by presence's `arrivals()` — the
  one function that knows who was moved by whom. `gave-way` carries no distance, because presence has
  already recorded the new position when bodies hears the arrival.

**Consequences.** Every world with bodies and walkers changes its facts (walks end off the threshold;
some blocked strides now give way), so bodies' version is bumped. Step-11's non-goal "local avoidance
of moving people" stays a non-goal: R2 moves only people who are not walking.
