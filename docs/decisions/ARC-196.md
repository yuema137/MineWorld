## ARC-196 — A 2D pack binds a place by its most specific tag set; end doorways face outward; a shaped room is drawn at its shape

**Date** 2026-10-19 · **Status** accepted (design frozen by the operator 2026-10-19, Q-S29f-1 … 6 as
recommended; implemented in PR `mvp0/pr-s29f`) · **Relates to** `ARC-45`, `ARC-46`, `ARC-82`, `ARC-105`,
`ARC-108`, `ARC-307`, [`clients/2d/PRESENTATION.md`](../../clients/2d/PRESENTATION.md) §§3.1, 4, 4.1 ·
**Design** `.structured-coding/plans/mvp0/pr-s29f-station-2d.md` §§5.2–5.6

**Problem.** Three facts stopped the 2D client from drawing the station honestly in any world.

1. **Tag order cannot tell a depot from a halt, nor a hall from its platform.** The client chose a
   façade and an interior by the first of a place's tags, in the order the world lists them. Every
   station place lists `station` first, so the 52 m depot, a village halt, the ticket office and the
   platform would all get one picture.
2. **A doorway at the end of a street was turned sideways.** The client assumed every hub doorway lines
   one of the street's long sides. The station closes the street's west end, on its axis, and was drawn
   north of the street, running along it.
3. **The client could not draw a room at its real size.** Rooms were drawn at the pack's guessed size,
   although `bodies` discloses the shape of the place the observer stands in.

**Options.** (a) A `halt` tag in world data: declined, a picture hint living in world content. (b) A
disclosure of a neighbour's size, so the façade could follow the station's size: declined, a contract
change (step-29 I-S29-1); a client knows only a neighbour's tags (`ARC-82`). (c) **Keys naming a set of
tags, the most specific matching set winning, plus disclosed shapes once inside**: chosen.

**Choice.**

1. **Tag set keys.** A `facade:` role, an `interiors`, `outdoor` or `facades` key may name several tags
   joined by `+` (`facade:station+landmark`, `station+platform`). A place matches a key when it carries
   every tag the key names. Of the matching keys, the one naming the most tags wins; among equals, the
   one whose first tag comes first in the place's own order (the rule before this one). `*` and
   `facade:unknown:<n>` are never matched this way. Example: a hall `[station, public, landmark]` takes
   `facade:station+landmark` (the depot); Lakeside's hall `[station, public]` takes `facade:station`
   (the halt), because the village's landmark is its pier. The order of a world's tags no longer changes
   a picture.
2. **End doorways face outward.** A hub doorway that alone holds an end of the hub's long axis, and lies
   more than 0.5 m inside both of its sides, leads outward along that axis. Every other doorway keeps the
   across-the-street rule.
3. **A shaped room is drawn at its shape.** When the observer has stood in a place whose observation
   carries `place-shape`, the room's rectangle is that floor, placed through the layout's translation; a
   room entered from inside runs from its door toward the floor's middle. A place with no disclosed shape
   keeps the pack's size. Fittings are still placed from the doorway by the pack (`ARC-45` point 5).
4. **Stateful fittings and words.** A fitting may name a field of `MineWorldStationState` (`state:
   kiosk | window`, drawn by `<role>:<value>` when bound) and a `label` (`board`, `window`) the client
   writes over it; a furnishing kind may do the same through the pack's `furnishings.states`. A room may
   lower its far walls (`wall_m`, an open platform). The state comes only from `ARC-108`'s rule.
5. **Place names, specific before general.** A doorway names its destination by the first entry of an
   ordered table whose tag the destination carries: `ticket-office`, `platform`, `station`, then the
   existing six.

**Consequences.** Every existing choice in the four reference worlds is unchanged: each shipped key
names one tag, each place matches at most one, and every existing doorway lies on a side of its street.
Lakeside's halt stands at the lane's east end, the near side of the isometric camera, so it is drawn as
an open room like every near-side place; the halt façade is shown wherever a non-landmark station stands
on the far side (Q-S29f-2). The façade seen from the street keeps the pack's footprint until the place is
entered, because a neighbour's size is not disclosed.
