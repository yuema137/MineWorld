## ARC-332 — Solids of different things may not overlap; solids of one thing share a `group`

**Date** 2026-10-19 · **Status** accepted (design frozen by the operator 2026-10-19, Q-S36-P5-1 and
Q-S36-P5-3 as recommended; implemented in PR `mvp1/pr-s36-p5a`) · **Relates to** `ARC-39` (amended),
`ARC-306`, `ARC-307`, `.structured-coding/plans/mvp1/pr-s36-p5-scene-layout.md` (the design),
`step-36-interpenetration.md` §4.5

**Problem.** Nothing checked one solid of a place against another. Both towns carried eleven
overlapping pairs. Eight were right: a chair tucked under its table overlaps the table's box by 14 to
63 mm, and a round terrace table is held as two crossed boxes. Three were content defects: a bollard
stood 88 mm inside a stone planter (twice, on the street), and the florist's window staging ran 197 mm
into the crate by the window. A plain "no two solids overlap" rule would have rejected both towns for
shapes that are right, and no rule at all let real defects through.

**Choice.**

1. An authored solid may carry a `group`: a slug (1 to 48 bytes of `a`–`z`, `0`–`9`, `-` and `.`, the
   grammar of a furnishing key and of `posture`'s sit group). Solids of one thing share it.
2. When a place's `body:` section is decoded, after `PlaceShape::checked` passes, every pair of solids
   is tested. Two whose footprints share an area greater than zero must share a `group` or name one
   `furnishing` (`ARC-307`: a furnishing is one piece, so its solids are one thing already). Touching
   along an edge or at a corner shares no area. Otherwise the place is refused, the message led by the
   code `bodies-solids-overlap`, naming both solids by index (from 0, in the order written), their
   overlap in millimetres east–west by north–south, and the remedy.
3. The `group` is **authoring data only**. `PlaceShape` does not store it, so `place-shaped`,
   snapshots, disclosure and every digest are unchanged by it. A `PlaceShape` decoded from a fact or a
   snapshot is not re-checked for overlaps; it was checked when authored.
4. Every point the server puts a person at — each person's start, both ends of every passage, each sit
   point — is held by `tools/cli/tests/layout.rs` to `R + GAP` (260 mm) from every solid and from the
   floor's edge, over every world in `worlds/` (step-36 O-7). Genesis keeps its own margins (starts at
   `R`); the test is the stricter authoring bar (Q-S36-P5-3).

**Content.** Both towns: each café table's solid and its two chairs' share a group (`seating.east`,
`seating.north`, `seating.back`); each terrace table's two boxes share one (`terrace-table.1` … `.3`).
The north row of stone planters on the street stands 200 mm further from the kerb, drawn and solid
together (the drawn bollard's base reached 185 mm into the planter's lip, more than the 88 mm the boxes
overlapped). The window staging ends 250 mm sooner, drawn and solid together, 53 mm clear of the crate.

**Amends `ARC-39`.** An authored solid's written form gains the optional `group`; the stored form is
unchanged.

**Not decided here.** Whether a `group` later becomes a stored, disclosed key. `ARC-307`'s furnishings
already name the pieces a client draws, so a group is needed only where two separate pieces interlock
(a chair under a table).

**Why.** The overlap that is right and the overlap that is wrong look the same to geometry; only the
author knows which boxes are one thing. Saying it once, in the data, makes the rule exact and keeps it
generic: no world, place or kind is named in the code.
