## 1. Goal

After this PR, a player of the 2D client in Market Town or Social Café who walks to the west end of the street
sees the station closing it: a Mission Revival depot with two tiled, domed towers either side of a tall arch,
drawn in the default pack's illustrated style. They walk in. The waiting hall is drawn at its real size, 51.8 m by
16.8 m, from the shape the world discloses, with rows of long oak benches, a kiosk and a departures board. The
board reads "No service yet". In Market Town the kiosk is open, because the hall discloses the newsstand's shop;
in Social Café it is shut, because nothing is sold there. Through the door marked "Ticket office" the window is
shuttered with a "Closed" plate; through the door marked "Platform" there is an open platform beside a track, with
no train.

In Lakeside the same three places are drawn as a village halt, never as the 52 m depot, because the Lakeside
station is not the village's landmark (the pier is). Nothing in the client names `market-town`, `lakeside` or the
key `station`: every choice is made from the role tags the world discloses and from the shape it discloses.

### 3.2 Findings

- **F-S29f-1. Tag order cannot tell the depot from the halt, nor a hall from its platform.** Every station place
  carries `station`, and the world lists it first (A-14). Today's rule (A-1, A-2) therefore gives one façade and
  one interior spec to all six station places of three worlds. The step's remedy, an extra `halt` tag (step §8.1),
  needs S29-a to author a tag that exists only to steer a sprite, and S29-a rev. 3 does not author it. The
  generic remedy is that **the most specific matching set of tags wins** (§5.2): `station+landmark` is more
  specific than `station`, and `station+platform` more specific than `station`. Lakeside's hall is not the
  landmark (Q-S29-3 ruled the pier), so it draws as the halt without any new tag.
- **F-S29f-2. The depot's doorway lies on the street's axis, and the 2D rule turns it sideways.** A-3's rule
  assumes every doorway lines a long side. A doorway at the end of the street, strictly between the two façade
  lines, is a new case. Concrete example, Market Town: the street's doorways span x −62.05 … +15.3 m and y −12.6 …
  +2.8 m; the hall's doorway (−62.05, −4.9) is the only one at the west extreme and lies 7.7 m inside each façade
  line, yet A-3 points it north. §5.3 adds the end-doorway case, which leaves every existing doorway unchanged
  (each lies on a façade line).
- **F-S29f-3. "By station size" can choose the interior, not the façade.** The client learns a place's size only
  from `place-shape`, and only for the place it stands in (A-6, A-19). From the street it knows a neighbour's tags
  (ARC-82) and nothing of its size. Choosing the façade by size would need the world to disclose a neighbour's
  extent — a change to `movement`'s passage disclosure or to `bodies`, which I-S29-1 forbids. So: the façade is
  chosen by role tags (§5.2); the room, once entered, is drawn at the disclosed size (§5.4). Q-S29f-1 asks the
  operator to confirm.
- **F-S29f-4. Lakeside's halt is on the near side, so 2D draws it as an open room, not as a façade.** The halt
  stands at the lane's east end (Q-S33a-1, ruled). East is "near" in the pack's isometric camera (A-4), and every
  near-side place is drawn as a cut-away room (the office in Market Town is drawn the same way). The halt sprite
  is therefore never shown in default Lakeside; it is shown wherever a non-landmark station stands on the far
  side, which the generated variant V1 proves (§8). Lakeside shows its halt as the open waiting room with its
  bench, board, shut kiosk and shut window. Q-S29f-2 asks the operator to accept this reading of AC-S29-7.
- **F-S29f-5. A client cannot see whether a shop's kind is tagged `ticket`.** The window rule (step §5.1,
  `ARC-108`) needs item kinds' tags. Shops disclose item ids only (A-18) and item kinds are never perceived
  (A-19). So the rule, evaluated honestly, keeps the window **shut** until something discloses a kind's tags.
  That is the true state before S21, and it stays fail-closed after. S21's RT-c must disclose the tags of the kinds
  its `fares` shop lists (R-RTc-f-1). The pure function still takes item tags as an input, so X-5's "opens" half is
  proved by its table check with a fixture, and the live drive proves the "stays shut" half. This affects S29-e
  equally (§10, the cross-PR item). Q-S29f-6.
- **F-S29f-6. A doorway would name the platform "Station".** A-8's rule takes the first naming tag in world order.
  §5.5 makes the table an ordered list, specific first (`ticket-office`, `platform` before `station`), which
  changes no existing label (A-8).
- **F-S29f-7. The scale normalizer never reached `main`.** Generated candidates come out at their own size; the
  cut to "pixel height = metres × 64" was done by a spike tool on an unmerged branch (A-13). §5.8 ports it into the
  existing generator package as `fit.py`, in Python with Pillow (already a requirement), with a test. Reuse: the
  spike's algorithm and constants, not a new method.
- **F-S29f-8. The depot is wider than the generator can draw at 64 px per metre.** The hall's frontage is 51.8 m.

- **F-S29f-9. The far-field stills move.** The hub's extent grows west by about 42 m in the two towns and east by

## 5. Design

### 5.1 The shared state rule (`ARC-108`): the interface both clients use

**File:** `clients/shared/station/station_state.gd`, symlinked as `res://mineworld_station` into
`clients/2d` (this PR) and `clients/3d-spike` (S29-e). **Check:** `clients/shared/checks/station_state_check.gd`.
This is the one implementation of the rule; neither client re-states it.

```gdscript
class_name MineWorldStationState
extends RefCounted

## ARC-108: a station fixture shows the state the world disclosed about the place the observer stands
## in, and nothing else. Pure: no clock, no file, no node, no request; a client draws the result.

const NONE := "none"      # board: nothing disclosed
const LINES := "lines"    # board: a departures record was disclosed
const OPEN := "open"
const SHUT := "shut"
const TICKET_OFFICE := "ticket-office"   # the role tag (CORE_CONCEPTS §6, ARC-105)
const TICKET := "ticket"                 # the item-kind tag a fare carries (S21 QRT-10)

## The fixtures of the observer's place, from one observation.
static func of(observation: MineWorldObservation) -> Dictionary

## The same rule from its parts — the form the table check calls, and the one a client uses when it
## holds the parts itself.
##   place_tags  the tags the world lists for the observer's place
##   departures  the place's disclosed `departures` payload, or null
##   shop        the place's disclosed `shop` payload, or null
##   item_tags   item id string → PackedStringArray of that kind's disclosed tags (absent: none known)
static func evaluate(place_tags: PackedStringArray, departures: Variant, shop: Variant,
		item_tags: Dictionary) -> Dictionary
```

**Result**, always all three keys:

```text
{ "board":  { "state": "none" }
          | { "state": "lines", "lines": Array },     # the disclosed entries, unchanged
  "window": "open" | "shut",
  "kiosk":  "open" | "shut" }
```

**Rules**, in this order, each reading only its inputs:

1. **Board.** `departures == null` → `{state: none}`. Otherwise `{state: lines, lines: L}`, where `L` is the payload
   itself when it is an Array, the payload's `lines` when it is an object holding an Array there, and `[]`
   otherwise. S21 RT-c fixes the record's shape (step §14.6); only the drawing of `L` can change then.
2. **Window.** `open` if and only if `place_tags` contains `ticket-office`, `shop` is an object whose `listed` is an
   Array, and at least one entry's `item` (an id string, or a typed reference `{entity}`) has `ticket` among
   `item_tags[item]`. Otherwise `shut`. With today's observations `item_tags` is always empty (F-S29f-5), so the
   window is shut everywhere until a disclosure carries kinds' tags.
3. **Kiosk.** `open` if and only if `place_tags` does not contain `ticket-office` and `shop != null`. Otherwise
   `shut`. The kiosk follows the place's disclosed shop, never an organization key (S29-b).

`of(observation)` builds the parts from the observation: `place_tags` from `observation.entity(place).tags`;
`departures` and `shop` from `observation.component_value(place, …)`; `item_tags` from
`observation.entity(id).tags` for every perceived entity of type `item` (none today). It reads no key, no name
and no wall clock.

**What it never does.** It never submits, never reads a place's key or a display name, never reads a component
of a place other than the observer's, and never treats "not disclosed" as "open". A client draws a fixture of a
place the observer does not stand in with `evaluate([], null, null, {})` — every fixture shut, the board blank
with "No service yet" — because nothing about that place was disclosed.

**The table check** (`station_state_check.gd`, headless, run from `clients/shared` like `far_field_check.gd`)
prints one line per case, `STATION <case> <board> <window> <kiosk>`, and a summary. The cases:

| Case | Inputs | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hall_nothing` | `[station, public, landmark]`, null, null | none, shut, shut |
| `hall_shop` (Market Town, S29-b) | same tags, null, `{operator, listed: [newspaper]}` | none, shut, open |
| `tickets_nothing` | `[station, ticket-office, public]`, null, null | none, shut, shut |
| `tickets_newspaper` (X-5, first half) | ticket-office, shop listing kind 9 with tags `[news]` | none, shut, shut |
| `tickets_ticket` (X-5, second half) | ticket-office, shop listing kind 9 with tags `[ticket]` | none, open, shut |
| `tickets_untagged` (F-S29f-5) | ticket-office, shop listing kind 9, `item_tags` empty | none, shut, shut |
| `departures_planted` (X-7) | hall, `[{line: "coast"}]`, null | lines [1], shut, shut |
| `departures_empty` | hall, `[]`, null | lines [0], shut, shut |
| `variant_tag_order` | `[public, ticket-office, station]` with the ticket shop | none, open, shut |
| `variant_no_landmark` (Lakeside's hall) | `[station, public]`, null, null | none, shut, shut |

### 5.2 The most specific tag set wins (`ARC-196` item 1)

One function in `clients/2d/scripts/presentation.gd`, used for every tag-keyed lookup:

```gdscript
## The key of `table` (or of the active roles, when `prefix` is given) that best matches `place_tags`:
## among keys "<prefix><t1>+<t2>+…" whose every tag the place carries, the one naming the most tags;
## among equals, the one whose first tag comes first in the place's own order (the rule before this
## one). "" when no key matches. `*` is never matched here.
func best_key(keys: PackedStringArray, prefix: String, place_tags: PackedStringArray) -> String
```

Used by:

- `places.gd` `_facade`: the façade role is `best_key(bound_roles(), "facade:", tags)`. `facade:unknown:<n>` stays
  the fallback (unchanged).
- `places.gd` `_by_tag`: `interiors` and `outdoor` choose by `best_key(table.keys(), "", tags)`; `interiors` still
  falls back to `*`.
- `places.gd` `_facade_dressing`: `facades` by the same rule; `*` first, as today.

**Compatibility.** Every key in the shipped pack names one tag, and every existing place matches at most one of
them, so every existing choice is unchanged (A-8's audit holds for these tables too: `cafe`, `apartments`, `park`,
`residential` are the only keyed entries, and no shipped place carries two of them). `facade:unknown:<n>` is
excluded by name (`unknown` is not a tag a world may rely on; MODULE_SPEC already reserves nothing, so the
function skips keys whose first part is `unknown`).

**Example.** Market Town's hall `[station, public, landmark]` with bindings `facade:station` and
`facade:station+landmark` → `facade:station+landmark` (two tags beat one) → the depot. Lakeside's hall `[station,
public]` → only `facade:station` matches → the halt. A variant hall listed `[landmark, public, station]` → still
`facade:station+landmark`, because order only breaks ties.

### 5.3 End doorways face outward (`ARC-196` item 2)

`places.gd` `_inward(door, extent)` gains one case before its existing body:

```text
Let the long axis be x when extent.size.x >= extent.size.y, else y.
A doorway is an END DOORWAY when
  (a) its long-axis coordinate equals the extent's minimum or maximum on that axis (within 0.01 m), and
  (b) no other hub doorway has that same long-axis coordinate (within 0.01 m), and
  (c) its short-axis coordinate lies strictly inside the extent on that axis, more than 0.5 m from both edges.
An end doorway's inward direction is outward along the long axis: toward the extreme it holds.
Every other doorway keeps today's rule.
```

`_inward` becomes an instance method reading the hub's doorways (or takes them as an argument; the implementer
keeps it static with a third parameter). It is presentation only, like today's rule (`ARC-45` point 4).

**Results.** Market Town and Social Café: the hall's doorway (−62.05, −4.9) holds the west extreme alone and lies
7.7 m inside both façade lines → inward west → "far" → a façade (the depot). Lakeside: the hall's doorway
(29.6, 3.0) holds the east extreme alone and lies 2.8 m inside both lines → inward east → "near" → an open room
(F-S29f-4). Every existing doorway in the four reference worlds lies on a façade line (y 2.8 / −12.6 in the towns,
5.8 / 0.2 in Lakeside), fails (c), and keeps its direction.

`PRESENTATION.md` §4 "Anchoring" gains the sentence: "A doorway that alone holds an end of the root place's long
axis, between its two sides, leads outward along that axis."

### 5.4 A room the world shapes is drawn at its shape (`ARC-196` item 3)

When the observer stands in a place whose observation discloses `place-shape` (`bodies`), the room's rectangle is
the disclosed floor: `Rect2` spanning `town.to_plan(place, floor.min)` and `town.to_plan(place, floor.max)`
(normalized, since plan `+y` is south). This replaces the spec's `width_m` × `depth_m` for that place, both for a
façade place being entered and for `_draw_room_from_inside`. It is kept in a per-instance dictionary
(`shapes: place → Rect2`) in `town.gd`, learned in `learn()` and dropped by `reset()`, like `tags`.

- **Fittings** are still placed from the doorway by the spec (`along_m`, `in_m`); the spec is the pack's
  dressing, and footprints remain decoration (`ARC-45` point 5).
- **Doors** are drawn at their disclosed positions, as today (`_doors`), now on the true walls.
- **A world without `bodies`** (Lakeside until S33-b) discloses no shape; the spec sizes apply, as today.
- **A façade seen from the street** keeps the spec's footprint until the place is entered (F-S29f-3).

Example: in Market Town, standing in the hall, the room is exactly 51.8 × 16.8 m with its doors at the arch, the
ticket bay and the platform; in a generated variant with a 30 × 12 m hall it would be 30 × 12 m, from the same
code.

### 5.5 Place names: specific before general

`PLACE_NAME_KEYS` becomes an ordered Array of `[tag, key]` pairs, and `_place_name` returns the first pair whose
tag the destination carries (and whose key is worded). Order: `ticket-office`, `platform`, `station`, then the
six existing pairs in their current order. The keys stay written out (AC-SET-4). Today's places are named as
before (A-8). Example: from the hall, the doors read "door to Ticket office", "door to Platform", "door to Street";
from the street, "door to Station".

### 5.6 Stateful fittings, the board label, open platforms

Additions to the `interiors` spec format (`PRESENTATION.md` §4), all optional, all presentation:

```json
"fittings": [
  { "role": "prop:kiosk", "state": "kiosk", "along_m": -23.4, "in_m": 14.2 },
  { "role": "prop:ticket-window", "state": "window", "label": "window", "along_m": 0.0, "in_m": 3.9 },
  { "role": "prop:departures-board", "label": "board", "along_m": 3.1, "in_m": 16.4, "lift_m": 2.2 }
],
"wall_m": 1.1
```

- **`state`** names a field of the fixture state (`kiosk` or `window`). The role drawn is `<role>:<value>`, e.g.
  `prop:kiosk:open` or `prop:kiosk:shut`. If that role is unbound the fitting falls back to `<role>` (plain if
  that too is unbound).
- **`label`** draws a `Label` over the fitting: `board` → `Words.text("station.no_service")` when the board's state
  is `none`, else one line per entry of `lines` (the entry's `line` field when it is a string, else the entry as
  JSON, readers.gd's rule); `window` → `Words.text("station.window_closed")` when the window is `shut`, else no
  text. Label style follows the door labels (`places.gd:418-428`).
- **When state is read.** On every `reconcile`, `places.gd` computes `MineWorldStationState.of(observation)` once,
  for `here`. Stateful fittings and labels of the place the observer stands in follow it; those of any other place
  use `evaluate([], null, null, {})` (§5.1, "nothing disclosed"). A change of state re-resolves only those
  fittings; it does not rebuild the town.
- **`wall_m`** (default 2.8) is the height of the room's far walls; near stubs stay `min(wall_m, 0.42)`. `0`
  draws a floor with no walls. `ground.gd` `_room` reads it from the area.
- **No new rule.** These keys choose a picture and a word; the state comes from §5.1. The client-rules scan's R4
  and R6 hold (§3.1 A-21); the drive asserts that nothing is submitted by drawing (§7).

### 5.10 Ownership, determinism, platforms

- **Ownership.** Nothing here owns world state. The client draws what the Observation carries. The pack's data
  chooses pictures; `MineWorldStationState` chooses fixture states from disclosures; both are presentation
  (`ARC-46`, `ARC-47`).
- **Determinism.** The drawing is a function of the observation and the pack. Edge planting keeps its seeded
  jitter. The generator is not deterministic (`seed: null` in sidecars); its outputs are committed files, so the
  client is.
- **Platforms.** GDScript, PNG and the shared module run the same on Linux, macOS and Windows; the symlink needs
  `core.symlinks` on Windows (A-17, as for the existing shared modules). The generator runs on the operator's
  machine only.
- **Agent view.** Untouched; S29-g renders the same tags (step §9.4).
